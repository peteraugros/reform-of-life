Practice five

Fasting

Not a diet and not a test of willpower. It is a way of telling the truth with your body about what you actually need, and it is the oldest of these practices by a long way.

Part one

What it is for

Hunger is honest. You can talk yourself into almost any belief about what you depend on; going without food for a day tells you the truth in about four hours.

That is most of the point. Fasting makes an appetite visible by refusing it, and once one appetite is visible the others are easier to see. It is not that God wants you hungry. It is that a person who has never once said no to himself has no idea whether he can.

The second reason is older and stranger: fasting is joined to prayer everywhere in scripture, as though the two work on each other. Nobody has ever explained that well. People who do both report that it is true.

And the third is that what you do not spend on yourself can go to someone else. A fast that saves nothing and gives nothing away is only a diet with better branding.

Part two

What the Church actually asks

Less than most people assume, and it is worth knowing precisely, because a vague sense of obligation is heavier to carry than a real one.

Two days a year

Fasting

The two days are Ash Wednesday and Good Friday. You eat one full meal, plus two smaller ones that together do not add up to another full meal, and you do not eat between them. This binds Catholics from 18 to 59; if you are pregnant, ill, or your work makes it unsafe, you are excused, and that is the rule rather than a loophole.

Fridays in Lent, and two other days

Abstinence from meat

This covers every Friday of Lent, plus Ash Wednesday and Good Friday. Fish is allowed, which is why the fish fry exists. This binds everyone from 14 up.

Outside Lent, every Friday of the year is still a day of penance, which surprises people. In the United States the bishops allow you to substitute some other penance for going without meat. What is not on offer is doing nothing at all; Friday is the day Christ died, and the Church has never let that pass unmarked.

Before communion

One hour

Nothing but water and medicine for the hour before you receive. Since it counts from when you receive rather than when Mass starts, this is usually already true by accident.

Part three

How to start

One Friday. Not Lent, not a rule for the rest of your life, one Friday coming up.

  1. 1
    Pick the Friday, in advance

    Decided on Tuesday is a fast. Decided at noon on Friday when you happen not to be hungry is nothing. The deciding beforehand is half of what makes it work.

  2. 2
    Choose one thing, and be specific

    You might go without meat, or eat nothing between meals, or take nothing but bread and water until dinner. Vague fasts collapse quietly around four in the afternoon, because there is nothing definite to have broken.

  3. 3
    Attach it to something

    Fast for a particular person or a particular thing. It stops being an endurance exercise and becomes an argument you are making with your whole body.

  4. 4
    Give away what it saved

    Actually give it, in money or in the hour you did not spend on lunch. This is the part that is usually dropped and it is the part that turns the fast outward.

  5. 5
    Tell nobody

    Christ is unusually blunt about this. A fast you have mentioned has already been paid for.

When it goes badly

You will be irritable, and you will notice how much of your day was arranged around small comforts you did not know were there. That is not the fast failing. That is the fast working, and it is unpleasant.

If you break it, eat the meal and start again next Friday. A broken fast is a fact about a Friday, not a verdict on you.

Fasting is the soul of prayer, and mercy is the life of fasting. Let no one try to separate them; they cannot be divided. St. Peter Chrysologus
