Practice one

How to Pray the Rosary

Everything you need is in your hands already. The beads keep count so your mind is free to do the actual work, which is thinking about the life of Christ.

Part one

What you are holding

A rosary is a counting tool. Each bead is one prayer, and the shape of the string tells you which prayer comes next. You never have to remember your place, which is the whole point.

The parts, in order

The crucifix is where you begin and end.

The short strand runs one bead, then three together, then one more, which is five beads carrying you up to the medal.

The medal, or centerpiece, is the join. You announce each mystery here at the start, and you finish here at the end.

The loop holds five sets of ten beads. Each set of ten is called a decade, and each decade gets one mystery. The single beads between them mark the change.

If you do not have one

Any rosary works, including a free plastic one from the back of a church, and plenty of saints wore theirs out. But a rosary you like the feel of gets prayed more often than one you do not, which is less sentimentality than an observation about objects you carry in a pocket.

The Catholic Woodworker makes them by hand from wood and cord, built to be carried and pulled at rather than kept in a drawer. A rosary that survives being sat on is worth more than a delicate one you leave at home.

Part two

Praying it, bead by bead

Red says what to do. The rest is what you say. If you lose your place, you have lost nothing; pick up the nearest bead and keep going.

  1. 1
    Hold the crucifix

    Make the Sign of the Cross, then pray the Apostles' Creed.

  2. 2
    First bead

    Pray one Our Father.

  3. 3
    The next three beads

    Pray three Hail Marys, one on each. They are traditionally offered for faith, hope, and love.

  4. 4
    Before the medal

    Pray one Glory Be.

  5. 5
    At the medal

    Say which mystery you are on. Out loud or in your head: "The First Joyful Mystery: the Annunciation." Then take a moment to picture it.

  6. 6
    The single bead

    Pray one Our Father.

  7. 7
    The ten beads

    Pray ten Hail Marys, one on each, thinking about that mystery as you go.

  8. 8
    End of the decade

    Pray one Glory Be, then the Fatima Prayer.

  9. 9
    Repeat four more times

    Announce the next mystery, Our Father on the single bead, ten Hail Marys, Glory Be and Fatima Prayer. That is five decades, five mysteries, once around the loop.

  10. 10
    Back at the medal

    Pray the Hail, Holy Queen and the closing prayer, then make the Sign of the Cross. That is a complete rosary.

How long it takes

About twenty minutes, unhurried. It is meant to be slow. Rushing it is a bit like sprinting through a song to get to the end.

Part three

The part that is not the words

This is the thing nobody explains, and it is the difference between praying the rosary and reciting it.

The Hail Marys are not the point. They are the rhythm underneath the point. While your mouth is saying them, your mind is somewhere else on purpose: inside one scene from the life of Christ, looking at it.

So for the Annunciation, you are not thinking about the word "annunciation." You are picturing a teenage girl, alone in a room, being asked something impossible, and saying yes. Stay there for ten beads. That is the whole exercise.

If your mind wanders, and it will, come back when you notice. Noticing and coming back is the prayer. Nobody does this without wandering.

Each mystery below carries a scripture reference, if you want to read the passage first, and a fruit, which is the thing that scene is asking of you. Neither is required. On a hard day, saying the words at all is enough.

Part four

The Mysteries

There are twenty scenes in four sets of five. Each set has a traditional day, though the days are a custom rather than a rule; pray whichever set you need.

Monday & Saturday

The Joyful Mysteries

The coming of Christ, and the ordinary lives it interrupted.
  1. I
    The Annunciation Luke 1:26–38 · Humility
  2. II
    The Visitation Luke 1:39–56 · Love of neighbor
  3. III
    The Nativity Luke 2:1–20 · Poverty of spirit
  4. IV
    The Presentation in the Temple Luke 2:22–38 · Obedience
  5. V
    The Finding of Jesus in the Temple Luke 2:41–52 · Joy in finding Christ
Thursday

The Luminous Mysteries

The public ministry. Added by St. John Paul II in 2002, so an older rosary book may not have them.
  1. I
    The Baptism in the Jordan Matthew 3:13–17 · Openness to the Spirit
  2. II
    The Wedding at Cana John 2:1–11 · Trust in Mary's intercession
  3. III
    The Proclamation of the Kingdom Mark 1:14–15 · Repentance
  4. IV
    The Transfiguration Matthew 17:1–8 · Desire for holiness
  5. V
    The Institution of the Eucharist Matthew 26:26–28 · Adoration
Tuesday & Friday

The Sorrowful Mysteries

The Passion. These are the hardest to sit with and often the ones that help most.
  1. I
    The Agony in the Garden Matthew 26:36–46 · Sorrow for sin
  2. II
    The Scourging at the Pillar John 19:1 · Purity
  3. III
    The Crowning with Thorns Matthew 27:27–31 · Courage
  4. IV
    The Carrying of the Cross Luke 23:26–32 · Patience
  5. V
    The Crucifixion and Death Luke 23:33–46 · Perseverance
Wednesday & Sunday

The Glorious Mysteries

What came after, and what it opens for everyone else.
  1. I
    The Resurrection Matthew 28:1–10 · Faith
  2. II
    The Ascension Acts 1:6–11 · Hope
  3. III
    The Descent of the Holy Spirit Acts 2:1–13 · Love of God
  4. IV
    The Assumption of Mary Revelation 12:1 · A holy death
  5. V
    The Coronation of Mary Revelation 12:1 · Trust in her prayers
Part five

The Prayers

All of them, in full. You will know most of these by heart within a week of praying it daily.

The Sign of the Cross

In the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit. Amen.

The Apostles' Creed

I believe in God, the Father almighty, Creator of heaven and earth; and in Jesus Christ, his only Son, our Lord; who was conceived by the Holy Spirit, born of the Virgin Mary; suffered under Pontius Pilate, was crucified, died, and was buried; he descended into hell; on the third day he rose again from the dead; he ascended into heaven, and is seated at the right hand of God the Father almighty; from there he will come to judge the living and the dead. I believe in the Holy Spirit, the holy catholic Church, the communion of saints, the forgiveness of sins, the resurrection of the body, and life everlasting. Amen.

The Our Father

Our Father, who art in heaven, hallowed be thy name; thy kingdom come; thy will be done on earth as it is in heaven. Give us this day our daily bread; and forgive us our trespasses as we forgive those who trespass against us; and lead us not into temptation, but deliver us from evil. Amen.

The Hail Mary

Hail Mary, full of grace, the Lord is with thee. Blessed art thou among women, and blessed is the fruit of thy womb, Jesus. Holy Mary, Mother of God, pray for us sinners, now and at the hour of our death. Amen.

The Glory Be

Glory be to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit. As it was in the beginning, is now, and ever shall be, world without end. Amen.

The Fatima Prayer

O my Jesus, forgive us our sins, save us from the fires of hell, and lead all souls to heaven, especially those in most need of thy mercy.

Hail, Holy Queen

Hail, holy Queen, Mother of mercy, our life, our sweetness, and our hope. To thee do we cry, poor banished children of Eve. To thee do we send up our sighs, mourning and weeping in this valley of tears. Turn then, most gracious advocate, thine eyes of mercy toward us, and after this our exile show unto us the blessed fruit of thy womb, Jesus. O clement, O loving, O sweet Virgin Mary. Pray for us, O holy Mother of God, that we may be made worthy of the promises of Christ. Amen.

Closing Prayer

O God, whose only begotten Son, by his life, death, and resurrection, has purchased for us the rewards of eternal life: grant, we beseech thee, that meditating upon these mysteries of the most holy Rosary of the Blessed Virgin Mary, we may imitate what they contain and obtain what they promise, through the same Christ our Lord. Amen.

Last thing

If you are just starting

Pray one decade rather than five. One decade is about four minutes and it is a real rosary prayer, not a lesser version of one. Most people who try to begin with the full twenty minutes stop within a couple of weeks.

Do it at a fixed time so you are not deciding each day whether to. You can pray it in the car, walking somewhere, or in bed with the light off.

And keep the beads where your hands will find them. That is most of it. The rosary in a drawer is the one that does not get prayed.

The Rosary is my favorite prayer. A marvelous prayer, marvelous in its simplicity and its depth. St. John Paul II
