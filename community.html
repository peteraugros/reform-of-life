Practice eight

Community

Everything on this site so far can be done alone in a room, and none of it was meant to be. This is the practice that is hardest to start and the one that decides whether the others last.

Part one

Why alone does not hold

A private faith is easier, more comfortable, and reliably temporary. That is not a moral claim, it is an observation about what happens over a few years.

Practices kept alone drift, and they drift in the direction you were already going, because there is nobody positioned to notice. You do not get told when your version has quietly become smaller. The correction has to come from people who know you well enough to say something and are close enough that you will hear it.

And the shape of the thing itself assumes company. Nearly every prayer in the Mass is in the first person plural. The whole account is of a people rather than a set of individuals with a shared interest, and a person praying "our Father" alone every day is saying something slightly odd.

Part two

The parish, which is the awkward part

Everyone knows they should and almost nobody does, because a parish is a room full of strangers who all appear to know each other, and walking into that at thirty is worse than at seven.

Three things that make it survivable.

Go to the same Mass every week

The same time, the same building. Recognition does the work that introductions cannot; after about six weeks people start nodding at you, and after a few months somebody says something. This costs nothing but consistency and it is the single most effective thing on this page.

Stay for ten minutes

The coffee afterward is nobody's idea of a good time and it is where the entire social life of a parish happens. Ten minutes, one conversation, then leave. You do not have to be good at this.

Say yes to the first thing you are asked

Someone will eventually ask you to help with something, and it will be inconvenient and slightly beneath your skills. Say yes anyway. Doing an unglamorous job beside somebody is how adults actually become friends, and there is no faster route in.

Part three

The two or three

A parish is the wide circle. What you also need is small: two or three people who know what you are actually trying to do.

Not a program, and it does not need a name or a curriculum. It is a couple of people you can tell the truth to, who will ask how it is going and mean it, and who will notice if you disappear for a month.

How this usually forms: you are honest with one person first. That is the whole mechanism, and it is the frightening part, because the first person who hears that you have started praying again might react badly. Occasionally somebody does. Far more often they say something you did not expect about themselves.

Bring someone with you

The last step, and the one that changes you rather than them. Ask one person to come to something. They will probably say no, and asking is still the thing that turns you from someone receiving this into someone handing it on.

Which is, in the end, how you came to be reading this. Somebody handed it to you.

Where two or three are gathered together in my name, there am I in the midst of them. Matthew 18:20
