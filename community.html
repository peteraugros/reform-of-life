Practice eight

Community

Christ did not call individuals to follow Him privately. He gathered a people, and He gave us the Church.

Part one

Why we cannot do this alone

Everything on this site up to now can be done alone in a room. None of it was meant to be.

A faith kept entirely to yourself is easier and more comfortable, and over a few years it usually thins out. That is not a moral judgment. It is simply what tends to happen.

Practices kept alone drift, and they drift in whatever direction you were already going, because there is nobody near enough to notice. You need people who know you well enough to say something and are close enough that you will actually hear it.

The shape of our prayer assumes company. Nearly everything we say at Mass is in the first person plural. Christ taught us to say our Father.

Part two

The parish

Almost everyone knows they should, and almost nobody does, because walking into a room where everyone seems to know each other is harder at thirty than it was at seven.

Three things make it easier.

Go to the same Mass every week

The same time, the same building. Recognition does what introductions cannot. After a month or so people begin to nod, and eventually somebody says something.

This costs nothing but consistency, and it is the most effective thing on this page.

Stay for ten minutes

The coffee afterward is nobody's idea of a thrilling event, and it is where the life of a parish actually happens. Ten minutes, one conversation, then go. You do not have to be good at this.

Say yes the first time you are asked

Someone will eventually ask you to help with something inconvenient and unglamorous. Say yes.

Doing an ordinary job beside someone is how adults become friends, and there is no faster way in.

Part three

Two or three people

The parish is the wide circle. You also need something small: two or three people who know what you are trying to do.

It does not need a name or a program. It is a few people you can be honest with, who will ask how it is going and mean it, and who will notice if you disappear for a month.

This usually begins with telling one person the truth. That is the frightening part, because they might react badly. Occasionally someone does. Far more often they tell you something about themselves you did not expect.

Bring someone with you

The last step is the one that changes you rather than them. Ask one person to come to something.

They may well say no. Asking is still what turns you from someone receiving this into someone handing it on.

If you want something to put in their hands, a rosary is the traditional answer and hard to refuse; The Catholic Woodworker makes good ones. That is a personal suggestion rather than an endorsement, and nothing comes back to me for it.

Which is, in the end, how you came to be reading this. Somebody handed it to you.

Where two or three are gathered together in my name, there am I in the midst of them. Matthew 18:20
