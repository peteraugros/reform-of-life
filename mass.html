Practice four

How to Follow the Mass

It has the same shape every week, everywhere in the world. Once you can see the shape, you stop watching for cues and start actually being there.

Part one

Two halves and a hinge

Strip away the details and the Mass is two things joined together, with a short opening and a short ending on either side.

The Liturgy of the Word. Readings from scripture, then a homily. God speaks first.

The Liturgy of the Eucharist. Bread and wine are brought up, the great prayer of thanksgiving is prayed over them, and communion is given. This is the part everything else is arranged around.

That pattern is very old and it is not arbitrary. It is the same order as the road to Emmaus in Luke 24: first he explains the scriptures to them, then he breaks bread, and only then do they recognize him.

Part two

Start to finish

Red is what is happening. Below it, what you do.

About five minutes

Introductory Rites

  1. 1
    Entrance

    Everyone stands and sings as the priest comes in. Before that, if you can arrive a few minutes early, kneel and say why you came.

  2. 2
    Greeting

    Sign of the Cross, then the first of the exchanges you will hear all through.

  3. 3
    Penitential Act

    A short admission that you have not been what you should be. Say it as though you mean it; it is only about a minute, and it is the door into the rest.

  4. 4
    Gloria, then the opening prayer

    The Gloria is sung on Sundays outside Advent and Lent. Then a short prayer that gathers everyone's intentions together.

About twenty minutes

Liturgy of the Word

  1. 5
    First reading, psalm, second reading

    Sit. Usually Old Testament, then a psalm everyone answers, then a letter from the New Testament. Listen rather than read along if you can; it was written to be heard out loud.

  2. 6
    Gospel

    Everyone stands, and there is a small gesture worth knowing: a thumb-sized cross traced on your forehead, your lips, and your heart. On my mind, on my lips, in my heart.

  3. 7
    Homily

    Sit. Take one thing from it. One is a good result.

  4. 8
    Creed and intercessions

    Stand. The Creed on Sundays, then prayers for the Church, the world, the sick and the dead. Add your own silently.

About twenty-five minutes

Liturgy of the Eucharist

  1. 9
    Preparation of the gifts

    Sit. Bread, wine, and the collection are brought forward. Traditionally you put something of your own week on the altar in your head at this point; whatever it is you are carrying.

  2. 10
    The Eucharistic Prayer

    Stand for the opening exchange and the Holy, Holy, Holy, then kneel. This is the center of the whole hour: the account of the Last Supper is prayed over the bread and wine, and Catholics hold that they become the body and blood of Christ. Kneeling here is the oldest instinct in the room.

  3. 11
    Our Father, sign of peace, Lamb of God

    Stand for the Our Father. Then turn to the people near you and wish them peace, which is meant sincerely and not as a formality. Then a short litany while the bread is broken.

  4. 12
    Communion

    See part five below. Afterward there is a stretch of silence, and it is the most valuable silence of the week; do not spend it looking around.

Two minutes

Concluding Rites

  1. 13
    Blessing and dismissal

    Stand. A blessing, then you are sent out; the word "Mass" comes from that sending. It is not "you may now leave," it is "go and do something with this."

Part three

Standing, sitting, kneeling

Each posture means something. Standing is respect and readiness, sitting is listening, kneeling is worship and repentance.

  • Stand for the entrance, the Gospel, the Creed and intercessions, the Our Father through the sign of peace, and the final blessing.
  • Sit for the readings, the psalm, the homily, and while the gifts are prepared.
  • Kneel through the Eucharistic Prayer, and again after the Lamb of God until it is your turn to go up to communion.

These vary a little from one diocese and parish to another, and nobody minds. The reliable rule is to do what the people around you are doing, half a second behind them. That is not cheating; it is how everyone learned.

Two smaller ones: genuflect, which is a brief drop of the right knee, toward the tabernacle before you enter your pew; and bow your head briefly before receiving communion.

Part four

The responses you will need

The priest's line is in italics; yours follows. There are only about a dozen and you will have them within a month.

The Lord be with you.
And with your spirit.
A reading from the holy Gospel according to…
Glory to you, O Lord.
The Gospel of the Lord.
Praise to you, Lord Jesus Christ.
The word of the Lord.
Thanks be to God.
Lift up your hearts.
We lift them up to the Lord.
Let us give thanks to the Lord our God.
It is right and just.
Pray, brethren, that my sacrifice and yours…
May the Lord accept the sacrifice at your hands, for the praise and glory of his name, for our good and the good of all his holy Church.
The peace of the Lord be with you always.
And with your spirit.
Behold the Lamb of God…
Lord, I am not worthy that you should enter under my roof, but only say the word and my soul shall be healed.
The Body of Christ.
Amen.
Go forth, the Mass is ended.
Thanks be to God.

That last-but-two response is worth knowing about rather than just saying. It is the line the Roman centurion says in Matthew 8, a man who was not a Jew, asking for a healing he did not consider himself entitled to, and being commended for the greatest faith in Israel.

Part five

Communion

Rows go up in turn; follow the people in front of you. When you reach the front, bow your head briefly, then either put your left hand over your right and receive in the hand, or open your mouth and receive on the tongue. Both are permitted; do whichever you find less awkward.

The minister says "The Body of Christ." You say "Amen" out loud, and then consume it right there, before you turn to walk back. Then return to your pew, kneel, and stay quiet.

When not to receive communion

Two situations, and neither is a judgement on you.

  • If you are aware of serious sin you have not yet confessed. Go to Confession first, then receive next time. This is the ordinary sequence, not an exile.
  • If you are not Catholic, or are not in a position to receive for another reason.

In either case you can still go up: cross your arms over your chest and the priest will give you a blessing instead. It is a normal thing to do and nobody watching thinks anything of it.

The fast

Nothing but water and medicine for one hour before receiving. Most people manage this without trying.

Last thing

Getting more out of it

Find one that fits your week. masstimes.org lists Mass times for parishes across the country, including Saturday evening and Sunday evening, which most people do not realize count. It is also the thing to use when you are away from home; there is almost always a Mass somewhere near, and turning up somewhere you have never been is a perfectly normal way to go.

Read the Gospel before you go. Ten minutes on Saturday night, and Sunday stops being the first time you have met the passage. Any parish website or missal app has the day's readings.

Arrive five minutes early. The difference between sliding in at the entrance hymn and sitting quietly beforehand is larger than five minutes should account for.

Bring one thing to put on the altar. A person, a decision, something you are afraid of. Offer it during the preparation of the gifts. It gives the hour a spine.

Expect to be distracted. Everyone is, every week, including the saints. Coming back when you notice is the same work as in the Rosary, and it is not a sign you are doing it badly.
