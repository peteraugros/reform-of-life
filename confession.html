Practice three

How to Go to Confession

Almost nobody stays away because they disagree with it. They stay away because they do not know what to say, and are not willing to find out in front of someone. So here is the script.

Part one

Before anything else

Three things are true, and if you only take these away the rest will sort itself out.

The priest has heard worse. Not as a figure of speech. He has heard your sin, many times, from many people, and it did not land as a shock. Whatever you are carrying that feels uniquely disqualifying is, from where he is sitting, Tuesday.

He cannot tell anyone. Ever. The seal of confession is absolute, without exception, at any cost to himself. There is no circumstance in which what you say leaves that room.

You do not have to know how. "Father, it has been a long time and I have forgotten how this goes" is a completely normal way to begin, and he will walk you through it. Priests genuinely like it when someone comes back after years away. It is the best part of the job.

Part two

Beforehand: ten quiet minutes

Sit somewhere on your own and think back over the time since you last went. You are not building a legal case against yourself. You are trying to see honestly.

Some questions that get at it faster than a list does:

  • Where have I put something else in God's place; money, work, my phone, someone's opinion of me?
  • Who have I hurt, and did I go back and repair it?
  • Where have I been dishonest, including the kind nobody catches?
  • What have I used another person for?
  • What good did I clearly see I should do, and not do?
  • What am I hoping not to have to say out loud tomorrow?

That last one is usually the one that matters. Write them down if it helps; you can take the paper in with you, and plenty of people do.

How far back

Since your last confession. If that was fifteen years ago, then fifteen years, and you say so at the start; he will help you shape it rather than expecting a complete inventory. Anything genuinely serious should be named. The smaller things are worth bringing too, though they are not the reason the sacrament exists.

Part three

What you actually say

This is the whole thing, in order. Red is what happens; the quoted lines are yours.

  1. 1
    Go in

    There will be a screen you can kneel behind, and usually a chair if you would rather sit face to face. Either is entirely normal, and it is your choice, not his.

  2. 2
    Open with this

    Make the Sign of the Cross, and say:

    "Bless me, Father, for I have sinned. It has been [a month / two years / a very long time] since my last confession."

  3. 3
    Say what you came to say

    Plainly, without building a case around it. "I lied to my parents about where I was." "I have been sleeping with my girlfriend." "I have not spoken to my brother in two years and that is my doing." No explanation is required and no performance is wanted.

  4. 4
    Close the list

    When you have finished:

    "I am sorry for these and all my sins."

  5. 5
    He talks; then he gives you a penance

    Usually a short word of counsel, then something small to do afterward: a few prayers, sometimes an action. Listen for it, because you will want to remember it. If you do not catch it, ask him to repeat it.

  6. 6
    He asks you to make an act of contrition

    The traditional one is below. If you do not know it, say so; many confessionals have it printed on a card, and in your own words is also fine.

  7. 7
    He absolves you

    He will extend his hand and pray the words of absolution, ending "I absolve you from your sins, in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit." You answer:

    "Amen."

  8. 8
    He sends you out

    Often "Go in peace," to which the answer is "Thanks be to God." Then you leave, find a pew, and do your penance. That is the entire thing, and it usually takes under five minutes.

Part four

The Act of Contrition

The traditional form

O my God, I am heartily sorry for having offended thee, and I detest all my sins because of thy just punishments, but most of all because they offend thee, my God, who art all good and deserving of all my love. I firmly resolve, with the help of thy grace, to sin no more and to avoid the near occasion of sin. Amen.

A shorter form, equally acceptable

My God, I am sorry for my sins with all my heart. In choosing to do wrong and failing to do good, I have sinned against you, whom I should love above all things. I firmly intend, with your help, to do penance, to sin no more, and to avoid whatever leads me to sin. Amen.

In your own words is genuinely fine. What has to be there is that you are sorry and that you mean to try.

Part five

The things people worry about

"I forgot to say something."

If you forgot it honestly, it is forgiven along with the rest. Mention it next time if it stays with you. Confession is not a form that gets voided by an omission.

"The priest knows me."

Then go to a different parish; nobody will think anything of it, and priests do this themselves. But it is worth knowing that being known has not once made a priest think less of anyone in that chair. It usually does the opposite.

"I will only do it again."

Probably, and that is not a reason to stay away. The resolution is to try, seriously, not to guarantee an outcome. Anyone who has kept at this has confessed the same thing many times. That is what the monthly rhythm is for.

"It has been so long that it would be embarrassing."

Twenty years is not an obstacle to a priest. It is the thing he hoped would happen this week.

"How often should I go?"

Once a month is the usual counsel, and it works because it is often enough that you can remember, and regular enough that you stop dreading it. The minimum obligation is once a year if you are conscious of serious sin, but a minimum is a poor thing to aim at.

"When can I go?"

Most parishes hear confessions on Saturday afternoon. To find the times near you, wherever you are, use masstimes.org; it lists confession times alongside Mass times, and it covers parishes across the country, so it works when you are traveling too.

You can also just call the parish office and ask for an appointment. That is completely ordinary, and for a first time back after years away it is often the easier route: no queue behind you, and as long as you need.

Last thing

Afterward

Do your penance before you leave the building if you can, while you still remember it.

And then let it be over. Turning it back over afterward, re-examining whether you said it properly, is a way of taking it back. It is done. That is the entire promise of the thing.

There is no one so bad that he cannot become good. There is no one so lost that he cannot be found. Attributed to St. John Bosco
