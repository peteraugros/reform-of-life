Practice seven

Service to Others

Christ told us that what we do for the least of His brothers, we do for Him. He meant it literally.

Part one

The works of mercy

The Church has kept a list for centuries, and the list is useful precisely because it is concrete.

"Be a good person" cannot be done on a Tuesday afternoon. These can.

For the body

The corporal works of mercy

  • Feed the hungry
  • Give drink to the thirsty
  • Clothe the naked
  • Shelter the homeless
  • Visit the sick
  • Visit the imprisoned
  • Bury the dead

Nearly all of these have an address somewhere near you. Your parish either runs something or knows who does, and one phone call will tell you.

For the soul

The spiritual works of mercy

  • Counsel the doubtful
  • Instruct the ignorant
  • Admonish sinners
  • Comfort the afflicted
  • Forgive offenses
  • Bear wrongs patiently
  • Pray for the living and the dead

These are harder, because you cannot schedule them and you cannot volunteer for them. They arrive. The work is recognizing one when it does, and not looking away.

Part two

Three things worth keeping in mind

Let it cost you something

Giving away what you had no use for is tidying. When Christ pointed out the widow at the treasury, He was not measuring the size of her gift but what she had left afterward.

You do not need to be dramatic about this. But if nothing about your week is different, nothing has really been given.

Do it quietly

Christ is blunt about this: give in such a way that your left hand does not know what your right hand is doing.

Not because being seen is a sin, but because praise is a complete payment, and once you have taken it there is nothing left to receive.

So choose something nobody in your life will hear about.

Serve a person, not a cause

Causes are easier. A cause has no opinions, never disappoints you, and is never tedious. A person is specific, often difficult, and frequently not grateful.

Writing a check is genuinely good and you should do it. But go and be with somebody as well, because that is what Christ did.

Part three

Starting this week

Not a commitment. Do one thing, once, and then decide.

  • Call the parish office and ask what they need. It takes four minutes, and they are always short of people.
  • Visit someone who is not going to get better. A hospital, a care home, someone from your parish who stopped appearing. This is the one people avoid, and it is the one that changes them.
  • Take an hour at a food pantry or a shelter meal. Concrete, finite, and it puts you in a room you would otherwise never enter.
  • Repair something you broke. Forgiveness and bearing wrongs are on the list too. That call you have been avoiding counts.

Why this belongs with the rest

Prayer that never reaches your neighbor curdles. St. James put it bluntly: faith without works is dead.

The practices on this site are not a private arrangement between you and God. They are meant to make you someone who can be relied upon.

Whatever you did for one of these least brothers of mine, you did for me. Matthew 25:40
