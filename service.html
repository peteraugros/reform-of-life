Practice seven

Service to Others

The test the Gospel actually names is not what you believed or how you prayed. It is whether you fed anybody. That is uncomfortable enough to be worth taking literally.

Part one

The works of mercy

The Church has kept a list for centuries, which is useful precisely because it is unromantic. "Be a good person" cannot be done on a Tuesday. These can.

For the body

Corporal works

  • Feed the hungry
  • Give drink to the thirsty
  • Clothe the naked
  • Shelter the homeless
  • Visit the sick
  • Visit the imprisoned
  • Bury the dead

Most of these have an address near you. Parishes run or know about all of it, and the office will tell you in one phone call.

For the person

Spiritual works

  • Counsel the doubtful
  • Instruct the ignorant
  • Admonish sinners
  • Comfort the afflicted
  • Forgive offenses
  • Bear wrongs patiently
  • Pray for the living and the dead

These are harder, because none of them can be scheduled and most of them cannot be volunteered for. They happen to you. The work is recognizing one when it arrives.

Part two

Three rules that keep it honest

Service goes wrong in predictable ways, and all three of these are aimed at a specific failure.

It has to cost something

Giving from what you had no use for is tidying up. The measure in the Gospel is not the size of the gift but what the giver had left afterward, which is why the widow with two small coins outgives everyone in the building.

You do not have to be dramatic about this. But if nothing about your week is different, nothing has actually been given.

Nobody needs to know

Christ is direct about this to the point of bluntness: do it where it cannot be seen. Not because being seen is a sin, but because applause is a complete payment, and once you have taken it the transaction is closed.

The practical version: pick something nobody in your life will ever hear about.

It is a person, not a cause

Causes are easier. A cause has no opinions, never disappoints you, and cannot be tedious. A person is specific, often difficult, and frequently not grateful. Every serious tradition on this says the same thing: go and be with somebody, because writing a check is good and it is not the same act.

Part three

Starting this week

Not a commitment. One thing, once, and then decide.

  • Call the parish office and ask what they need. This is the single highest-yield phone call available to you, and it takes four minutes. They always need something and they are always short of people.
  • Visit somebody who is not going to get better. A hospital, a care home, a person from your parish who stopped appearing. This is the one people avoid, and it is the one nearly everybody reports as having changed something.
  • Take an hour at a food pantry or a shelter meal. Concrete, finite, needs no skill, and it puts you in a room you would otherwise never be in.
  • Repair something you broke. Forgiveness and bearing wrongs are on the list too. The person you have not spoken to counts, and that call is service even though nobody would describe it that way.

Why it belongs beside the other three

Because prayer without it curdles. A practice that only ever goes inward produces a person who is very serious about God and no use to anyone, and the tradition is unanimous that this is a recognized failure mode rather than an unlucky outcome.

Whatever you did for one of these least brothers of mine, you did for me. Matthew 25:40
