Practice six

Prayer

Prayer is not a performance. It is speaking to God, who already loves you and already knows.

Part one

Speaking in your own words

The Rosary gives us words to pray when we don't know what to say. But sometimes we need to speak to God in our own words.

You don't have to sound holy. Tell God what is actually happening in your life. Tell Him what you are grateful for, what you are afraid of, where you are angry, where you have failed, and where you need His help.

He already knows. Prayer is how we learn to bring it to Him.

If it feels strange at first, that is normal. Most people who pray this way felt awkward when they began. Keep going.

And if you think your complaints are too irreverent to say out loud, read the Psalms. They are the Church's own prayer book, and they contain grief, anger, fear and open protest. God can take what you actually feel.

Part two

The Examen

The Examen is a short prayer at the end of the day, in five movements. It takes about ten minutes and needs nothing but quiet.

St. Ignatius of Loyola gave it to his companions and told them that if they had to let everything else go, they should keep this.

  1. 1
    Give thanks

    Name two or three particular things from today and thank God for them. Particular, not general: the phone call, the sunlight, the fact that the thing you dreaded was smaller than you expected.

  2. 2
    Ask for light

    Ask the Holy Spirit to help you see the day as it really was, rather than as you have already explained it to yourself.

  3. 3
    Walk back through the day

    From morning until now. Where was God present? Where did you turn toward Him, and where did you turn away? You are not judging yourself. You are looking with Him.

  4. 4
    Ask forgiveness

    There will be something you would rather pass over. Name it simply and ask for mercy. This takes a minute, not an hour.

  5. 5
    Look toward tomorrow

    Think of one thing you can see coming, and ask for the grace to meet it well.

Pray it for a week and you will begin to notice patterns in your life that you have been living inside without seeing. That is not a technique working. That is God showing you your own life.

Part three

Time before the Blessed Sacrament

Many parishes have adoration at some point in the week, and many keep a chapel open longer than you would expect.

You sit, or kneel, in front of the Blessed Sacrament and stay there. Bring nothing, or bring the Gospel you are reading.

There is no technique to learn. Your mind will wander a great deal, you will come back, and the time will end. The first few times it may feel like nothing is happening.

Go anyway. Christ is present there, whether or not you feel anything, and being in His presence is not measured by what you get out of it. Almost everyone who has kept this up says the same thing: the hours that felt emptiest turned out to matter.

Last thing

When prayer feels like nothing

There will be stretches, sometimes long ones, when prayer is flat and you suspect you are talking to the ceiling.

Every serious writer on the spiritual life describes this, and they say the same thing about it: do not change your practice while you are in it. Keep the same time and the same length.

Praying only when it is enjoyable is a different arrangement than the one Christ offers. Staying when it is dry is a kind of faithfulness, and it is worth more than the days when it comes easily.

Distraction is not failure either. Your mind wanders; you come back. Coming back is the prayer, exactly as it is on the beads.

Prayer is nothing else than being on terms of friendship with God. St. Teresa of Avila
